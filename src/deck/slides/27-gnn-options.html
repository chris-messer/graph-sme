Three ways in, ordered by cost, and the cheapest one involves no GNN at all

Both of the source’s solutions here are high complexity, so the order matters more on this need than on any other in the deck. Climb only as far as the decision actually requires.

Extension · not from the source Cheapest way to test the hypothesis
Feed graph features into the model you already run Compute centrality, community id and node embeddings in the batch job you already have, write them to Delta, and add them as columns to the production model. No new training stack, no new serving path.
FitsA model is already in production and the open question is whether structure adds signal at all.
FailsThe decision turns on the shape of a neighborhood rather than a summary of it. One number per node discards exactly what a GNN would use.
TradeoffNothing new to operate, and a hard ceiling on how much structure you can express.
Pragmatic first move High · 4–8 weeks
Fine-tune a pretrained graph foundation model Ingest to Delta, align per-element features in Spark, export training shards, fine-tune a pretrained model on a GPU cluster, benchmark. Or coreset training — the 1 to 10 percent of the data chosen by embedding and clustering. Demonstrated end to end on public DFT data.
FitsScientific and atomistic work, where a pretrained model already exists for the domain and the physics is shared.
FailsNo pretrained model exists for your domain. A business network has no equivalent of an interatomic potential to inherit.
TradeoffMost of the accuracy at a fraction of a from-scratch pipeline — and you inherit the pretrained model’s assumptions with it.
Only where the graph is the decision High · 8+ weeks · served from MLflow
Build a purpose-made heterogeneous GNN Define the heterogeneous schema, build feature extraction from telemetry, train, register in MLflow, serve. Worked example: partition-skew optimization, where schema, runtime telemetry, query history and key distribution across seven node types feed a model that recommends the physical layout, down to which hot keys to salt.
FitsThe graph is the decision input, and the decision recurs often enough to amortize a trained model.
FailsStructure is one feature of a larger decision. Then this is the row above, or the row above that.
TradeoffDecisions learned from structure that heuristics miss, against the longest build in this deck and a model someone owns for years.

Reading this slideThe source names the order plainly — fine-tuning or coreset training are the pragmatic first moves, and a purpose-built heterogeneous GNN earns its complexity only where the graph is the decision input itself. It does not offer a cheaper rung than those two, which is why the top band is marked as our extension. It is frequently enough to answer the question, and it costs a batch job.
