            What the benchmark measured
            
On a controlled seven-question retail-hierarchy benchmark, the closure room answered five of seven with the lowest average SQL complexity of any encoding that answered correctly; the adjacency room answered two of seven and produced no recursive CTE at all. Adding the segment table resolved the cross-branch question every encoding had missed. Small dataset, so read it as query shape rather than throughput.

          

          

            What it costs
            
A closure table stores one row per ancestor–descendant pair, so it grows with depth as well as node count, and it has to be rebuilt or incrementally maintained every time the hierarchy is restructured. Each segment definition needs a named owner. Both are worth pricing explicitly on a deep or frequently reorganised hierarchy.
