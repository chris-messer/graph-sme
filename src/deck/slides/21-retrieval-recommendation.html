One default, one condition that earns a climb, and one line not worth crossing without evidence. Most retrieval asks are answered in the first lane, on the deterministic seed from the slide before it.

Hop tables and recursive CTEs over one edge table, served through Genie

Genie writes recursive queries against the edge table directly, and pre-generated closures make the cost of the questions you ask most often quotable in advance. Most traversal asks never leave this lane.

Climb when Medium · two to four weeks

Your benchmark questions are structured, graph-shaped, and have to be deterministic

Compile Text → SPARQL → SQL over the same edges for roughly one-second answers a reviewer can read, and route the open-ended questions to the agentic path instead. This is still not an engine — the SPARQL compiles down to SQL.

Only past this line Medium–high · four to eight weeks

A graph engine or a partner database, once a benchmark query has actually failed

A demonstrated failure, plus one of three conditions: graph applications already in production, deep Cypher or SPARQL expertise on staff, or a real sub-second traversal requirement at high concurrency. Delta stays the system of record and the edges sync outward.

Model serving endpoints have no Spark Any engine served from an endpoint needs an in-memory execution path of its own, and anything heavy is precomputed in batch to Delta and served as a cheap lookup.
The seed and the depth are separate budgets The resolver decides whether the answer is right. The depth decides what it costs. Damp the supernodes explicitly, or one shared registered-agent address prices every traversal on the platform.
